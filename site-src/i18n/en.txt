Why the 1.8 V regulator matters
@@ e8b70b707f
The MN864739 needs 0.9 V and 1.8 V. The RTD2175P it replaces needs 0.9 V and 3.3 V, and that 3.3 V trace already exists on the board. The <strong>U1 (TI TLV71318PDBVR)</strong> regulator takes that 3.3 V and steps it down locally to 1.8 V.
@@ 4da7cbb08d
This way the interposer does not need to pull 1.8 V from another point of the motherboard. The HDMI chip is one of the parts that fail most in the PS5 video section: if the 1.8 V came from another point, such as the APU PMIC (CXD), an overload would have a free path to reach that circuit too. With the regulator on the part itself, that failure path stays contained there.
@@ ec51d86670
The regulator <strong>EN</strong> pin is on the 0.9 V trace: the 1.8 V output only rises once the 0.9 V is present, with no external component. This matches the <a href="https://www.ti.com/lit/gpn/TLV713" target="_blank" rel="noopener">TI datasheet</a>: EN turns on from 0.9 V and turns off below 0.4 V.
